TikTok's docs say posting to your own account needs an audit: privacy policy, a recorded demo, weeks of review. Until you pass, every post is forced private.

I found a door that skips all of it.

TikTok has two ways to publish. One goes live with no human involved, fully unattended. That's the one the audit guards.

The other drops the finished post into your account's own inbox as a draft. You tap the notification on your phone and publish it yourself. That path needs no audit, and it's not a lesser version: a draft delivered that way, from an app I never submitted for review, comes out as an ordinary, fully public post.

I didn't take TikTok's word for it. I checked the post from TikTok's own public, unauthenticated endpoint, no login, no token. Flagged as public. Real account, real post, zero review.

The real rule: TikTok's own-account gate isn't "no access." It's "no UNATTENDED access." Posting to your own account with a human tapping publish is free. The audit only buys two things: posting to OTHER people's accounts, and removing the human tap.

Same shape holds across TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, and X. LinkedIn's actually the friendliest of the lot: no partner review needed at all for your own profile. The question that decides whether a platform is cheap or expensive to automate isn't reach or reputation. It's just: can I post to my OWN account without a review?

Two portal traps that cost me real hours, in the full write-up: why the draft never shows up in your Drafts folder, and why a config form's "saving" progress bar can lie to you completely.
