Two weeks ago I posted about the player on our puzzle game who never lost.

It ended: "The scoreboard's clean again. For now."

For now lasted one day.

He came back — and the thing I learned in the two weeks since is the part I got wrong in the first post.

Catching cheaters doesn't work. Not because you can't catch them. We could, every single day. It doesn't work because **every time you catch someone, you teach them.**

He'd adjust. We'd catch the adjustment. He'd adjust again. Each round our rule got longer and more specific, and each round he learned the shape of the net by feeling where it wasn't.

That's not security. That's a subscription — and he sets the billing cycle.

Then the game accidentally lied to him.

He tried the trick without signing in at all, and hit a piece of leftover plumbing that handed him a completely wrong number. He spent that morning cracking a code that wasn't the code, carried it triumphantly back to his real account, and posted his worst result in ten days.

We hadn't built a trap. We'd left one lying around.

So we stopped asking "how do we spot him" and started asking "what is he actually taking?"

He was taking the ending. So we removed the ending from everywhere cheap to reach. Anyone not signed in now plays a warm-up round — a real puzzle, same rules, same feel, just not the ranked one. Scout it all you like. The number you walk away with was never the answer to anything.

Detection is rent. This was a purchase.

Two things I didn't see coming:

**1. Building the fix found worse than the cheat.** Six places in the game were still counting practice runs as real results — including the one that posts our daily best score publicly. Plus a leak nobody was exploiting, that nobody had noticed, sitting quietly in another room.

**2. The fix bit somebody innocent.** A week in, a player's sign-in check hung on their phone. No error, no retry — they simply looked, to us, like a stranger. And we had just made "looks like a stranger" mean "give them the practice puzzle." Real account, real streak, morning spent on a game that counted for nothing.

Which is the sentence I'd keep if I could only keep one:

**The day you make being unrecognised a punishment, every bug that fails to recognise someone becomes a punishment too.**

Is it over? No. One route still works, we know exactly what it is, and closing it would punish every genuine new player on day one — so we've left it open on purpose.

The scoreboard is clean along the routes we removed. Smaller claim. Truer one.
