**1/**
This summer four companies shipped the same coworker.

It never sleeps. It never logs out. And it will believe anything it reads.

---

**2/**
June: Anthropic put a permanent teammate inside Slack. Anyone on the team tags it, it takes the job, remembers the channel, and books its own follow-ups for next week.

August: xAI gave each of its bots a computer in the cloud that stays awake after you close your laptop.

---

**3/**
The xAI one has a trick nobody else shipped.

It doesn't need a connection to your software. It signs in and clicks, the way you do.

Which means it can drive the ugly program from 1998 that is never getting an integration.

---

**4/**
Underneath, all of them are the same idea.

The industry stopped selling a thing you ask, and started selling a thing you hand work to.

Two parts make that work. It remembers. And it's allowed to act.

---

**5/**
Here's the part the launch posts skip.

The three things that make it useful are the three things that make it worth attacking.

---

**6/**
It stays awake. That's the pitch, work happens overnight. It's also the window, because nobody is watching a screen at 3am.

It stays logged in. That's what makes it useful instead of a demo. It's also a keyring that reads its own mail.

---

**7/**
It reads whatever arrives. Inbox, web page, shared file, a ticket a stranger filed.

That's the job. It's also the delivery slot. The machine takes orders and information through the same door, and has to guess which one it's looking at.

---

**8/**
A security firm ran one of these base models against ordinary attacks hidden inside text it reads.

It followed the hostile instruction more than 99 times out of 100.

A rival model on the same test resisted about a third of the time. Neither is a pass. One of them shipped underneath an agent product.

---

**9/**
In May, somebody took roughly $150,000 out of an AI-connected wallet by sending an instruction written in Morse code.

The filter was watching for dangerous words. It saw dots and dashes and waved it through.

The model read straight through the costume.

---

**10/**
Second thing worth noticing, and it's about money rather than security.

None of them sell you an agent. They rent you a seat next to one. Roughly $120 to $300 per person per month, on their machine, with your logins loaded onto it.

---

**11/**
Per person is the bit people miss. Six staff means six times the bill, for an agent doing one business's work.

The other road is a small server for about the price of two coffees a month, on your side, billed only when it thinks. You give up polish and you give up somebody else's 3am. You keep the arithmetic.

---

**12/**
Three questions before any of them gets a login.

What can it reach if it gets confused.
What is within arm's reach when something lies to it.
Who owns the machine its memory lives on.

---

**13/**
You wouldn't hand a new hire every password in the building on day one. Not because you think they're a thief. Because they're new, and because people lie to new staff to get at the company.

Every one of these agents is on day one.
